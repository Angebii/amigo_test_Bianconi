**Use of AI tools**

**Tools and use.** I used Claude (Anthropic), through the Claude app, mainly to write code. It wrote the functions and scripts from my specifications, one step at a time, and I reviewed, ran and tested every step before moving on. I also used it to discuss methodological choices and to revise the report. The design decisions, the checks on the data and the final text are mine. The cases below are those in which the assistant was wrong, incomplete or not supported by a source, together with the way I found the problem.
**Corsica yields described as estimates.** When exploring the Agreste data, the assistant suggested excluding Corsica because its yields "are estimates" and "all 21 values are multiples of 0.5 t/ha", and presented both statements as facts. The Note sheet of the workbook does not document any estimate, so I checked the values myself. 20 of the 21 yields lie on a 0.5 t/ha grid, while 2003 is 1.8 t/ha, production equals area times the rounded yield, and 2002 and 2004 are identical. These patterns do not prove estimated values, and the numerical claim of the assistant was wrong. Since removing a region on an unproven assumption would change the dataset without a documented reason, I removed no region a priori.
**Silent error in the season completeness check.** The assistant wrote code that joins the monthly regional climate with the yield anomalies and keeps only the seasons with all ten months from October to July. The code ran without errors, but it returned 273 region-years from 2000 to 2020, while I expected 247 from 2001 to 2019. The reason is that precipitation covers 1991-2022 and the other variables only 2000-2019, and the check counted rows, so months with precipitation alone looked complete. Without this check, the seasons 2000 and 2020 would have entered the models with missing temperatures. I added a filter that keeps only the months where every variable has data before counting, and the result now matches the expected values.
**Deep learning model too large and trained without learning curves.** The first network proposed by the assistant had 8 filters, justified by the number of climate indices of the baseline, for a total of 417 trainable parameters, while each inner training set contains about 156 region-seasons from only 12 years, so it proposed a too much complex model for the dataset available. In addition, its training loop chose the number of epochs on the inner validation folds but did not record the training and validation losses. I asked for the training and validation loss of every epoch, as is standard practice in deep learning, and reduced the network to 2 filters and 10 pooling blocks.
**Input normalisation not discussed.** The assistant implemented an input normalisation that expressed every day of the season as a deviation from the normal of the same calendar day in the same region, computed on the training years and divided by one standard deviation per variable. When I reviewed the function, I found that each daily normal is a mean of only about 18 values and is therefore noisy. I replaced it with a standard normalisation, with the mean and standard deviation of each variable computed on the training sequences, which is simpler to justify and to reproduce. The RMSE of the network fell from 11.65 to 11.38 points of anomaly and the correlation rose from -0.16 to 0.14.
**Unsupported claim on the calibration period.** Before implementing the bias correction, I asked how to choose the calibration period against ERA5-Land. The assistant suggested joining the CMIP6 historical run, which ends in 2014, with the SSP5-8.5 run up to 2019, so that the calibration would match the whole study period, and it presented this as common practice. From my knowledge and my experience was not true. The bias-corrected CMIP6 products I checked calibrate on the historical experiment only. I therefore calibrate on 2000-2014, the overlap between the historical experiment and ERA5-Land.
**How I verified the output.** I compared intermediate counts with what I expected from the data (years, region-seasons, grid cells, days per season), read the learning curves and the printed tables at every step, and checked that every number of the report comes from a saved file or a printed output. For every methodological suggestion I asked for a published source, which I then read myself before the suggestion entered the pipeline.
